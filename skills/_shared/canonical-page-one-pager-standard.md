# Canonical Page One-Pager Standard

Apply this standard whenever writing or rewriting an ordinary canonical BigBrain page. The page must be a useful standalone briefing about the subject named in its title. A reader should be able to understand the subject's identity, significance, current state, important context, boundaries, material relationships, and meaningful uncertainty without opening another page.

The compiled-truth zone is the complete current page content above `---`. It starts with one concise opening summary paragraph and may include additional current-context sections. The append-only timeline is separate and belongs below `---`.

The page should give the best current 80/20 understanding of the subject. It should be comprehensive enough to be genuinely useful, but it should not become a competing full account of every linked subject or source. Write clear, natural prose. Use headings, bullets, tables, and other structure only when they improve comprehension. Do not force every page into a visible template.

Links belong inline where another subject is relevant to the explanation, and should point only to verified canonical targets. The link should clarify the sentence, not announce that another page exists. For example, an accelerator page can explain that when an accelerator is being exported, its technical characteristics may also be assessed using Total Processing Performance for purposes of export-control rules such as License Exception Low Processing Performance. The terms can link to their own explanations because they are relevant to the accelerator's export context.

Keep the page's subject in control of the scope. Include dependencies, counterparts, competitors, customers, regulations, technical constraints, evidence, and other context only when they help explain the subject. Do not reproduce the overall understanding of a linked subject.

For regulated or fast-changing subjects, identify the relevant authority, jurisdiction, effective date or rule version, and the boundary between confirmed source text, interpretation, and unknowns.

Do not include Brain taxonomy, page-type, graph, or linking-system commentary in an ordinary subject page. Avoid wording such as “this belongs to a different layer,” “this page should connect to,” “the reader can navigate to,” or “this concept links to.” Branch through meaningful subject context instead.

This full one-pager standard applies to canonical subject pages such as concepts, organizations, products, components, deals, and projects. Structured page types keep their own required shapes: task pages retain task metadata and handoff structure, meeting pages retain meeting context and preparation, and indexed attachment sidecars retain comprehensive source-specific extraction. Those pages must still be clear, subject-focused, and free of unnecessary page-system narration.

## Quality check

- Could someone read the page alone and understand what the subject is and why it matters?
- Does it explain the current position rather than only provide a definition?
- Are important relationships included in natural context?
- Do links add meaning at the point where they appear?
- Does every paragraph serve the subject named in the title?
- Have taxonomy commentary, page-system commentary, and unnecessary adjacent material been removed?
