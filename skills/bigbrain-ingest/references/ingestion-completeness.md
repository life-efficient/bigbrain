# Shared Ingestion Completeness Checklist

This is the single shared completion standard for every BigBrain ingestion route. Read it before writing, including when a subskill is invoked directly, and check it again before reporting completion. Source-specific skills own retrieval, relevance, source preservation, and specialized record shapes. Use `$bigbrain-page` for canonical subject pages.

For an accepted online article, including RSS and Substack, article ingestion is complete when the faithful full accessible substantive publisher body, or a substantive clearly marked partial preview, is saved as an indexed article in the owning Brain and independently read back with its identity, provenance, and access boundary verified. The article reading copy may omit only bounded promotional calls to action under [the article cleanup rule](../../bigbrain-source-article-ingest/references/reading-copy-cleanup.md); log those omissions and preserve unchanged fetched bytes separately when available. Canonical entity page creation and enrichment are separate follow-up work. Do not leave an otherwise verified article as `needs_review` because some mentioned entities lack pages, links, or updates. Classify the retained article by what was actually saved: `source_only` when only the article was saved, `source_and_update` when at least one supported canonical update was also verified, and `source_partial` for a verified substantive preview. A failed optional entity update is reported as a follow-up; it does not undo verified article ingestion. The more exhaustive canonical coverage requirements below continue to apply to other ingestion routes according to their source-specific contracts.

Apply this checklist to each source accepted for ingestion. A source rejected by its relevance gate requires no source or entity pages. Partial retrieval, unresolved identity, or failed verification must be reported as incomplete, not silently treated as full ingestion. A substantive, identity-confirmed partial preview may be retained when it passes the relevance gate, but only the readable evidence may be filed and the unresolved boundary must remain explicit. This checklist is an instruction for the AI to execute, not a required output schema, decision table, or separate tracking record.

## Partial preview protocol

- Use this branch only when the canonical page confirms the requested source and the readable portion is substantive enough to apply the normal relevance gate. Never use an email preview, RSS description, search snippet, or synthetic reconstruction as the captured source.
- Preserve exactly the readable source bytes in the raw artifact when available, and record that access stopped at a paywall or other block. The indexed article may omit only bounded promotional calls to action under the article cleanup rule, with each omission logged; it must not add or alter substantive text. Mark the raw artifact, sidecar, provenance, and any supported canonical updates as preview-only or incomplete.
- Read back every partial write through the owning Brain. Record a substantive relevant preview as `source_partial`, preserve the unresolved access boundary in the page and provenance, and advance the verified RSS, Substack, or Gmail watermark only after that read-back. Truly empty, ambiguous, or insufficient retrieval remains `needs_review`.
- If the readable portion is empty, thin, mismatched, or insufficient for a relevance decision, create no source or entity content and retain the item for retry.

## 1. Understand the whole source and its ownership

- For a full ingestion, read the complete available source, including substantive secondary sections, captions, and attributed speakers. A headline, summary, or primary subject is not sufficient. If access ends after a substantive preview, use the partial-preview protocol above and do not imply that the unseen source was reviewed.
- Resolve the owning Brain and read its live `filing_rules` before any write. Follow its collections, page shapes, raw-source storage, privacy, and provenance rules.
- Identify the source's subjects, every identifiable entity it mentions, and the facts, events, relationships, decisions, and reusable insights it establishes. Include people, authors or speakers, publishers, organizations, companies, funds, projects, facilities, places, and named concepts as applicable. Do not limit coverage to entities already in the Brain or central to the headline.
- For non-article sources, enumerate each candidate transaction, raise, investment, mandate, acquisition, or commercial opportunity. Distinguish an actual deal from meeting preparation, a meeting record, a VDR inventory, a diligence checklist, or another source artifact.
- Preserve exact source identity and distinguish publication date, event date, and retrieval date. A name or passing mention supports only that limited context, not an invented biography or relationship.

## 2. Resolve canonical pages before creating them

- Search the owning Brain for the source and any subject or entity page you intend to update, including aliases, abbreviations, spelling variants, and renamed entities. For non-article routes that require exhaustive coverage, search every identified entity. Read candidate pages to confirm identity rather than guessing from a title.
- Reuse the existing canonical page. Do not create a second page for an alias, a different source, or a new event involving the same subject.
- If a route requires a page for an identifiable mentioned entity and none exists, create a concise canonical page through `$bigbrain-page`, grounded in what the source actually establishes. For articles, create such pages when useful and supported, as separate enrichment rather than an article completion gate. For a partial preview, consider only entities identifiable in the readable portion and keep sparse evidence sparse and attributed. Do not infer entities or facts from the unseen remainder.
- For each actual deal established by a non-article source, search deal names, counterparties, and aliases, then create or update exactly one canonical `deals/` page when its identity is sufficiently clear. Set `deal_involvement` from evidence when the destination schema supports it. Keep prep on the source meeting page and VDR or diligence evidence in an owner-local `.raw/` sidecar rather than creating a second top-level deal page.
- If identity is ambiguous, preserve the mention and uncertainty in the source record, report the unresolved gap, and do not merge unrelated entities or invent an identity. Follow filing-rule restrictions and explicitly report any coverage they prevent.

## 3. Cover the knowledge without competing accounts

- For non-article routes that require exhaustive canonical coverage, make the full-source result mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive at the level of canonical subject ownership. For articles, preserve the full accessible publisher body on the article page; further canonical enrichment is independent. For a partial preview, save only the readable portion and explicitly record that the unseen content was not captured.
- On routes requiring exhaustive canonical coverage, update each existing entity page with the new supported facts, events, relationships, or context relevant to that entity. For articles, update pages that materially benefit from the source when doing separate enrichment. Integrate changes into current understanding and add dated evidence where appropriate; a link alone does not substitute for a factual update you claim to have made.
- When an entity page already covers everything the source establishes, reuse and link it without manufacturing a factual change or duplicating a timeline entry. Verify that coverage before treating it as a no-op.
- Keep each canonical page a useful briefing of its own subject. Keep source-specific detail on the source or specialized record, with links to deeper evidence and related subjects. Do not copy the same full account across company, person, project, and domain pages.
- Preserve existing knowledge, attribution, disagreement, and uncertainty. Do not strengthen what the source says or infer relationships beyond the evidence.
- Propagate relevant changes to existing domain or synthesis pages where the new evidence changes their understanding. Follow the route's action-review rules for supported tasks or commitments; do not invent tasks merely to make ingestion appear complete.

## 4. Connect source, entities, and related pages

- When the route requires a source or specialized record, create or update it and preserve raw material as required. Link an article to any canonical pages actually resolved or updated, without making links to every mention an article completion condition. Other routes may require links to every resolved entity under their source-specific contracts. For routes that retain evidence directly on canonical pages, such as human email or conversation ingest, preserve original-source references and provenance there; do not invent generic source logs or retain raw messages against the route's rules.
- Make supporting evidence reachable from each affected canonical page through an explicit source citation or a verified Brain backlink. New or changed factual statements must carry clear source attribution; a backlink by itself is not evidence for an unattributed claim.
- Link related canonical pages where the source supports the relationship, using the owning Brain's supported link format. For example, connect a person to their stated organization or a facility to its reported operator, without inventing a stronger relationship.
- Check that links resolve to actual canonical records and source artifacts. A bare name, unverified path, or link to a nonexistent page does not satisfy entity coverage.

## 5. Verify completeness from saved Brain state

- Independently read back every successfully created or updated record through the owning Brain before claiming that change. Confirm the intended content, source attribution, links, and preserved raw material where applicable. A successful write response alone is insufficient.
- For articles, reconcile the saved indexed article against the full accessible publisher body, or the exact readable preview, allowing only logged bounded promotional omissions under the article cleanup rule. Verify identity, provenance, access limits, and any canonical updates actually made. Do not require a page or link for every mentioned entity. For non-article routes requiring exhaustive canonical coverage, reconcile the whole source against the saved result: every identifiable mentioned entity has a canonical page created, updated, or verified as already covered and linked; each actual deal is represented by one canonical deal page or is explicitly held for identity ambiguity; each substantive fact has an appropriate home; and no needed canonical update remains only in a raw file, summary, or sidecar.
- Verify no-op claims against existing content and source identity. Re-ingesting the same evidence must not create duplicate pages, repeated facts, or duplicate timeline events.
- Rely on successful owning-Brain MCP writes for propagation and backup. Do not add manual sync, maintenance, or backup unless verification identifies a failed side effect.
- Repair gaps in required source capture and verification before declaring completion. If retrieval, identity, permissions, or verification prevents any reliable ingestion, report the specific remaining gap and keep the semantic outcome `needs_review`. For articles, report unfinished entity enrichment separately without holding the article open. A relevant partial preview may be saved and read back as `source_partial`, which is ingested with an explicit incomplete-capture note but is never presented as a full article.
- Finish with concise prose describing the source retained, canonical pages created or updated, existing coverage reused, verification, and any remaining gaps. Do not emit a structured decision output, internal outcome labels, or this entire checklist for the user. For RSS articles, follow the shared article skill's relevance explanation and one-paragraph summary requirements; this checklist does not replace them.
