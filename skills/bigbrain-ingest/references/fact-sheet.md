# Fact Sheet Standard

For an accepted source with substantial recall-worthy facts, place a short `## Fact Sheet` near the top of its indexed source page or sidecar, after identity/provenance and before the detailed source account. Omit the heading entirely when no fact meets the bar. This is an editorial index into the source, not a replacement for faithful source text, chronology, or canonical subject pages.

## Default: Numbers Only

Unless the user explicitly asks for quotes, themes, or a broader summary, a Fact Sheet contains only decision-useful quantitative claims. The useful unit is a number with its scale, unit, period, baseline or denominator, and source location. A memorable qualitative line does not qualify merely because it is quotable.

- Include quantities that reveal scale, change, rate, ratio, cost, capacity, timing, concentration, benchmark, or before-and-after performance. Preserve the unit and comparison: for example, `US$415B → US$820B year on year`, `1 GW → 6 GW`, `18x`, `500 bps`, or `90% lower`.
- Prefer figures that a reader is likely to recall and reuse months later: a spend level, growth rate, cost per unit, capacity, utilisation, margin change, market share, lead time, workforce change, or measured operating result.
- Use short labels to identify the subject and timestamp, but do not pad bullets with slogans, generic quotes, or qualitative commentary. A quote may appear only when it is essential to interpret the metric and is kept to the shortest useful fragment.
- Include units, denominator, geography, period, and comparison baseline wherever their absence would make a number misleading. State whether the figure is a disclosed result, source-reported claim, estimate, forecast, scenario, or anecdote.
- Exclude standalone qualitative claims or quotes, generic observations, rhetorical framing, investment slogans, routine biography and logistics, generic metadata, trivia, repeated talking points, and isolated numbers without context. `It is power`, `this feels like a bubble`, and `the beef is ROI` are not Fact Sheet items by themselves.
- Do not promote a source claim into a settled fact, manufacture a surprising angle, or fill the section for quota. For a partial source, draw only from the verified readable portion and label the limitation.

Example: `- Hyperscaler CapEx: US$415B → US$820B year on year across five companies, equal to 2.5% of US GDP, according to the speaker's presentation (05:30).`
