# Fact Sheet Standard

For an accepted source with substantial recall-worthy facts, place a short `## Fact Sheet` near the top of its indexed source page or sidecar, after identity/provenance and before the detailed source account. Omit the heading entirely when no fact meets the bar. This is an editorial index into the source, not a replacement for faithful source text, chronology, or canonical subject pages.

- Prefer a few self-contained bullets with a striking quantity, comparison, rate, threshold, ranking, counterintuitive finding, or concrete mechanism that changes how the reader understands a subject. Include units, denominator, geography, period, and comparison baseline when needed to make a number meaningful.
- Use a fact when it is likely to be useful months later for recall, explanation, or a decision. A source-specific fact may qualify even if it changes over time, provided its date or period is essential to the insight.
- Attribute each bullet to the speaker, author, or study and link to the precise source location when available: article section, page, chapter, or timestamp. Distinguish a source-reported claim, estimate, forecast, or anecdote from an independently verified observation. Keep material uncertainty and caveats in the bullet.
- Exclude routine biography and logistics, such as when someone last took a holiday; generic metadata; trivia whose only virtue is specificity; repeated talking points; vague claims; isolated numbers without context; and claims drawn solely from titles, snippets, descriptions, or inaccessible portions.
- Do not promote a claim into a settled fact, manufacture a surprising angle, or fill the section for quota. For a partial source, draw only from the verified readable portion and label the limitation.

Example: `- The speaker estimates that training runs use roughly 3x the power of inference at this facility in 2025; this is an estimate for one site, not an industry ratio (18:42).`
